**Aleksandar Lukić** (born 1970 in Belgrade) is a Serbian philosopher, researcher at the Institute for Political Studies in Belgrade, author of academic publications, and political commentator. According to a Ministry of Information and Telecommunications decision, he was **appointed a special adviser to the minister on 16 August 2025**. The reviewed documents do not establish whether he still held that position on 10 November 2026.[^1][^2][^3]

## Education and professional background

A 2019 biographical note published by Matica srpska — Society of Members in Montenegro states that Lukić earned his doctorate in philosophy at the University of Belgrade Faculty of Philosophy. He had participated in philosophical conferences, including the *Felix Romuliana* school, since 2006. His 2018 monograph **Obrnuti Vavilon — filozofsko istraživanje o Šelingovoj umnoj mitologiji** examines Friedrich Schelling's philosophy.[^1]

The Institute for Political Studies identifies him as a research associate and an organizer of the SoFoS symposium and the Gruža Philosophy Meetings. In June 2026, the institute also described him as president of the SoFoS Philosophical Society.[^2][^4]

In January 2026, Radio Television of Serbia (RTS) described Lukić as a long-serving editor of the Radio Belgrade 2 philosophy program **Gozba**. He appeared as a guest on its 26 January 2026 episode, authored by Nikola Tanasić. RTS credits Lukić as author and host of the 26 September 2016 episode.[^5] His 2019 biography also describes his work in art photography.[^1]

## Ministry of Information and Telecommunications

A document provided by the ministry to **Transparency Serbia** includes a decision dated **16 August 2025**, number **003491105 2025 13460 001 006 000 001**, appointing Lukić a special adviser to Minister **Boris Bratina**. The duties described include coordinating the minister's cabinet, communicating with ministries and state institutions, arranging protocol activities and carrying out tasks assigned by the minister.[^3]

On **4 December 2025**, the ministry reported that Lukić, identified as a special adviser, handed over 15 computers to a school in the municipality of Knić.[^9]

**Relevance to his public commentary.** Lukić held an academic role while having a documented appointment to a government minister's office. That connection is relevant context when assessing his comments on the government, elections and opposition. It does not, by itself, establish party membership or that he spoke at anyone's direction.

## Publicly documented political positions

### Russia, BRICS and international affairs

On **14 October 2024**, the publication *Bratstvo* posted speeches from an **Introduction to BRICS+** event held on **7 October** at the Russian House in Belgrade, organized by the Moscow–Belgrade International Eurasian Movement. The published text attributes to Lukić a speech titled *Istorija i budućnost BRIKS+* (“History and future of BRICS+”). It welcomes the growing importance of BRICS as a counterweight to Western dominance and expresses strong support for Vladimir Putin. The published text has not been independently checked against an original video recording.[^10]

In his **31 July 2026** article *Srbija i “sanitarni kordon” oko Rusije* (“Serbia and the sanitary cordon around Russia”), Lukić interprets European integration and NATO policy primarily through efforts to restrict Russian influence. These are the author's geopolitical views, not facts endorsed by this encyclopedia.[^11]

In *The New Geopolitical Perestroika* (2023), he presents world politics in terms of a confrontation between Russia and the “collective West” and the emergence of a possible counterweight led by Russia and China. His explanation of the war in Ukraine is attributed to him rather than treated as an established causal account.[^7]

### Serbian elections and the Student List

In an RTS interview on **8 September 2026**, Lukić argued that the Student List needed a clear political program, explaining what it supported rather than only whom it opposed.[^12]

After the **25 October 2026** parliamentary election, he told RTS on **27 October** that negative feelings toward the former government had substantially influenced the outcome and that the future government's policy was still unclear. In the same discussion, he condemned political violence regardless of its source and advocated reducing tensions.[^13]

On **10 November 2026**, RTS listed him as a research associate at the Institute for Political Studies among the guests of *Takovska 10*, discussing the formation of the new parliament. The announcement did not mention his earlier documented appointment to a minister's office. The difference in how his roles were presented does not establish intentional concealment.[^14]

## University of Belgrade appointment dispute, 2025

Lukić applied for an assistant professor position at the Faculty of Philosophy. After another candidate was selected, he challenged the procedure and evaluation of academic publications. Minutes dated **25 March 2025** show that the university's Council of Social Sciences and Humanities Scientific Fields voted unanimously, with **21 votes**, to recommend to the Senate that his appeal be rejected as unfounded. Its reasoning addressed the relevance of his publications to the advertised academic field.[^6]

This documents the council's assessment of the appeal, not a finding that Lukić lacks academic qualifications. A separate final decision by the Senate has not been verified here.

## What remains unverified

- **Membership in the Serbian Progressive Party (SNS):** no reliable confirmation of formal membership or a party office has been found in the reviewed materials.
- **Membership in the Eurasian movement:** attendance at an event it organized is documented, but membership is not.
- **Adviser status in November 2026:** the appointment decision and a 2025 ministry announcement do not establish continued service until that date.
- **Agreement with every position expressed by event organizers:** only Lukić's documented statements can be attributed to him.

A historian of the same name also works at the Institute of Contemporary History of Serbia. Their publications should not be conflated with those of the philosopher at the Institute for Political Studies.
