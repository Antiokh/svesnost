The survey starts in **1990**, before the SFRY completed its dissolution, and covers selected parties operating in Serbia through 2026. It is neither a complete register nor a survey of all former Yugoslav republics.

## 1990–1992: multiparty politics and federal dissolution

The breakup of Yugoslavia took place over several years, so the survey begins in **1990**, with Serbia's first multiparty parliamentary elections after World War II. On 9 and 23 December the Socialist Party of Serbia won 194 seats, the Serbian Renewal Movement 19 and the Democratic Party seven. Slobodan Milošević was elected president. The SFRY still existed at this point.[^1]

[Related event](/en/events/first-multiparty-election-1990/).

## Parties during the 1990s

[New Democracy (Serbia)](/en/parties/nova-demokratija-1990/) — registered in 1990; coalition list in 1997.


The SPS, SPO, DS, SRS, DSS, Civic Alliance of Serbia, Yugoslav Left and others participated in Serbian politics. **A coalition result is not a separate result for each member party.** For instance, the National Assembly records 110 seats for the joint SPS–JUL–New Democracy list in 1997.[^1]

## DOS and 5 October 2000

[Democratic Opposition of Serbia (DOS)](/en/coalitions/dos-2000/) — coalition, not a party.


The Democratic Opposition of Serbia (DOS) was **a coalition**, not a single registered party. After the disputed recognition of results from the 24 September FRY presidential election, mass protests took place on 5 October 2000. Vojislav Koštunica was the DOS presidential candidate and Slobodan Milošević the incumbent. Constituent parties followed separate trajectories afterward.[^2]

[Related event](/en/events/october-5-2000/).

## New parties and the 2008 split

Organisations such as G17 Plus and the Liberal Democratic Party emerged in the 2000s. In 2008 the Serbian Radical Party split, with Tomislav Nikolić and Aleksandar Vučić involved in founding the SNS. The SNS was registered on 10 October 2008 and held its founding assembly on 21 October; the SRS continued as a separate entity.[^3][^4]

[Related event](/en/events/sns-formation-2008/).

## 2010–2026: new organisations and legal continuity

The state register records entities such as the Green–Left Front, Serbia Centre, the Movement for Restoration of the Kingdom of Serbia and the Justice and Reconciliation Party. **The original legal registration date may differ from the date a party adopted its current name.** The People's Party adopted its name after a 2017 renaming of an earlier People's Movement of Serbia; a later organisation of that name has a distinct record.[^5][^6]

## Registration status and limitations

**A registered representative is not necessarily the party president.** Registered parties, coalitions, electoral lists and historical organisations are distinct categories. Related-party links are prompts for further investigation, not proof of legal succession.
