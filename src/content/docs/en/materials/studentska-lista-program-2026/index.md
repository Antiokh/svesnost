**Studentska lista — Studenti pobeđuju** (“Student List — Students Win”) was an electoral list formed by a citizens' group for Serbia's parliamentary election on **25 October 2026**. This article describes the list's **published election proposals**, not laws enacted or policies carried out. Its basis is the group's own eleven-point *Studentski plan* and its detailed programme covering twenty policy areas.[^1][^2]

The following is an **editorial synthesis, not a verbatim translation** of the programme. Each section refers to its official Serbian-language source. Specific thresholds and conditions are distinguished from broader political commitments. Last reviewed **14 November 2026**.

## The eleven priorities of the Studentski plan

**“Serbia or the Mafia”:** (1) judicial and prosecutorial independence, including a special prosecution police; (2) scrutiny and confiscation of unlawfully acquired assets and lustration of public officials; (3) open procurement and audits of state projects; (4) changes to media regulation and public broadcasting, including the regulator REM and broadcaster RTS.

**“Knowledge Is Power”:** (5) merit-based appointments and reduction of party control in the public sector; (6) modern education and free textbooks; (7) a knowledge-based economy supporting domestic businesses and agriculture.

**“Serbia Grows with Us”:** (8) indefinite employment contracts after twelve months; (9) curbing import-sector monopolies and improving food security; (10) affordable housing for young families and occupations facing shortages, through preferential **rental and purchase** schemes; (11) access to primary healthcare and medicines in villages.[^1]

## The twenty areas of the detailed programme

### 1. Economy, taxation and public finances

- Introduce more progressive personal income taxation calculated monthly; raise the tax-free portion of wages from 23% to 35% of the average wage.
- Assess property tax against an individual's combined holdings rather than each property separately; allow municipalities to impose an additional tax on flats vacant for more than twelve months.
- Raise taxes on large inheritances while exempting spouses, the family home and a substantial tax-free amount.
- Retain public control of strategic enterprises and appoint their management through professional selection procedures.
- Publish public contracts, tender prices and evaluations; blacklist contractors who breach public contracts for three years; review major projects and exceptional project legislation.
- Limit the budget reserve to 2% of revenue and require scrutiny of major state guarantees and public-private partnerships.
- Direct at least 30% of proceeds from confiscated assets to healthcare, research, talented children and social protection.
- Replace generic per-job investment subsidies with support linked to higher wages, subject to transitional exceptions in the poorest municipalities.
- Establish a Serbian development bank from existing financial institutions and double R&D spending by 2032.
- Require major retailers to publish daily store-level prices in machine-readable form; simplify administration for small businesses and implement the principle of submitting information to the state only once.
- Provide identity documents, health cards and electronic certificates free of charge; make public services available electronically by 2028 without abolishing in-person service.
- Maintain a relatively stable dinar and promote dinar-denominated savings and borrowing.[^3]

### 2. Infrastructure, housing, transport, telecommunications and IT

- Commission independent reviews of EXPO, Belgrade Waterfront, the Generalštab complex, Jadar and other large public investments; publish major contracts and investment registers.
- Prioritise repair and safety inspections of existing infrastructure over new megaprojects, including clearer professional and managerial liability.
- Keep public housing in a **non-profit rental stock that is not sold**; maintain registers of public and vacant housing and regulate short-term tourist lets.
- Standardise leases, protect tenants and prevent eviction from a sole home without a suitable alternative.
- Restrict construction in flood-risk zones, require property energy certificates and set minimum green-space standards.
- Introduce direct elections for local community councils and minimum proximity standards for schools, healthcare and transport.
- Extend fibre and mobile coverage to rural areas; integrate public transport fares and timetables and favour rail and public transport investment.
- Adopt **Cloud First** for government systems, move systems toward the state data centre in Kragujevac, keep control of critical data and keys in public hands and guard against dependence on a single IT supplier.
- Expand open data and Serbian-language AI models; develop AI rules aligned with the EU AI Act, cybersecurity rules aligned with NIS2 and a 2027–2031 cybersecurity strategy.[^4]

### 3. Employment and social protection

- Establish a general maximum of twelve months for fixed-term employment and no more than four successive contracts for the same role, subject to specified exceptions.
- Extend employment protections to temporary workers, platform workers and dependent self-employed workers.
- Guarantee meal and holiday allowances and higher premiums for public holidays, Sundays, night shifts, overtime and shift work.
- Provide a right to disconnect from work communications outside working hours and allow employers to adopt a **voluntary 32-hour, four-day week without reducing pay**.
- Raise standard sick-leave compensation during the first thirty days to 75%.
- Lower the threshold for trade-union representativeness to 5%, extend strike rights and establish specialised labour courts.
- Expand child benefits, social assistance and free school meals.
- Introduce a social pension after age 65 for people without sufficient contributions; improve support for family carers, veterans and surviving spouses.[^5]

### 4. Healthcare

- Restore rural health facilities and set minimum national standards for access to primary care.
- Create an integrated electronic health record and expand prevention, screening, school healthcare and palliative care.
- Use open professional competitions for hospital leadership; fund training and specialisations and offer incentives for medical professionals in small towns and rural areas.
- Expand publicly insured healthcare and dental services, potentially including agreements with private providers through Serbia's health insurance fund (RFZO).
- Set **one price for the same medicine across all pharmacies**, maintain access to state-owned pharmacies and require reserves of essential medicines.
- Develop community-based mental health and crisis-support centres.[^6]

### 5. Schools and early-childhood education

- Raise the base pay of education employees to **2.5 minimum wages** within three years.
- Increase spending on pre-university education from 3.4% to 4.4% of GDP.
- Provide free textbooks, a €100 allowance for vulnerable pupils and public support for school trips.
- Reduce administrative reporting; improve appointment procedures and provide scholarships for future teachers.
- Replace numerical grades with descriptive assessments during the first three grades and remove overall achievement categories.
- Expand public kindergartens and services for children with disabilities.[^7]

### 6. Universities and scientific research

- Give university and research-institute professional bodies the decisive role in selecting their leaders; use independent academic appointment panels.
- Offer permanent employment to eligible academic staff and restore institutional control of self-generated revenue.
- **Consider** reducing or abolishing tuition for self-funded students. The detailed text does **not** make an unconditional pledge to abolish all university tuition.
- Increase scholarships and student loans, with repayment beginning after employment; support specific higher-education institutions.
- Offer tax and customs relief for scientific equipment, discourage publication in predatory journals and support industry-linked doctorates.
- Encourage researchers to return from abroad and recognise foreign qualifications digitally within sixty days.[^8]

### 7. Agriculture

- Set legally enforceable subsidy-payment deadlines and interest on late payments; improve purchase contracts and prohibit unfair trading practices.
- Establish licensed warehouses, warehouse receipts and loans secured on produce; promote cooperatives, processing and storage.
- Protect agricultural land against inappropriate rezoning and foreign acquisition; support livestock farming.
- Subsidise farm-risk insurance, covering at least 50% of premiums for holdings up to 50 hectares.
- Establish a climate adaptation fund of at least **€50 million annually** and plant at least **10,000 hectares of forest each year**.
- Support young farmers, irrigation and cold storage and form an agricultural representative body.[^9]

### 8. Foreign policy and national interests

- Maintain military neutrality and Serbia's position that Kosovo forms part of its territory; protect the interests of Serbs in Kosovo.
- Support the institutional powers of Republika Srpska within Bosnia and Herzegovina.
- Continue European cooperation, including economic and legal reforms, while developing ties with the United States and China.
- **Decline to impose sanctions on Russia**, while affirming Ukraine's territorial integrity.
- Expand diplomacy with India, Africa and Latin America and provide humanitarian assistance to Palestine.[^10]

### 9. Defence

- Increase military pay and allowances and provide preferential housing loans for service personnel.
- Introduce voluntary six-month service for approximately **3,000 participants**, paid from 20,000 dinars per month, alongside an active reserve.
- Modernise air defence, aviation and logistics and develop military electronics and software.
- Investigate corruption in arms procurement and revisit selected deaths and accidents involving the military.[^11]

### 10. Mining and geology

- Apply international mineral-reserve classification standards and establish transparent permits, tenders and independent environmental assessments.
- Link mineral royalties to market prices and finance restoration and affected local communities.
- Encourage domestic mineral processing, strengthen inspection and allow greater community participation in project decisions.
- Provide retraining and employment support during mine closures or suspensions.[^12]

### 11. Energy

- Retain majority public ownership of strategic energy companies, with professional management.
- Decarbonise gradually while keeping thermal power plants in operation during the transition.
- Ban small diversion-type hydropower facilities that pipe streams away from their natural beds; promote household solar generation and energy communities.
- Subsidise insulation and energy efficiency and charge for district heating according to actual consumption.
- Expand gas interconnections and storage, diversify supply and preserve domestic oil refining.[^13]

### 12. Culture and copyright

- Allocate at least **1% of the national budget** to culture.
- Improve social insurance for independent artists and provide predictable core funding for cultural institutions.
- Seek at least 30% educational, cultural, documentary and children's content on national TV channels.
- Fund free cultural activities for children, use professional selection of cultural management and protect the Generalštab complex and Belgrade Fair site.
- Prohibit training AI models on copyrighted works without the rightsholder's consent, require labels for AI-generated content and compensate creators.[^14]

### 13. National minorities

- Make official forms, electronic services and administrative records available in minority languages where those languages have official status.
- Expand the use of Romani in public administration and ensure textbooks arrive on time in minority languages.
- Support minority cultural institutions and media, improve conditions in Roma settlements and reduce school segregation.
- Improve employment access and reform national minority councils.[^15]

### 14. Media

- Restrict reality-TV programmes on national free-to-air channels to **45 minutes per day, after 11 p.m., without live transmission**.
- Restrict editorial media ownership by a state-controlled telecommunications operator.
- Restructure the broadcaster RTS and regulator REM, including procedures for national broadcasting licences.
- Require transparency of media ownership and state-funded advertising.
- Protect journalists from threats and abusive lawsuits.[^16]

### 15. Justice, policing and elections

- Create a specialised prosecution police, increase judicial and prosecutorial budget independence, publish decisions and digitise case management.
- Introduce **lustration** of public officials, scrutiny of asset origins and potentially time-limited or permanent restrictions on holding public office after individual proceedings.
- Reform private enforcement officers and administrative courts while strengthening protection of a sole home.
- Transfer police internal oversight out of the Ministry of the Interior to the Ministry of Justice; introduce body cameras and demilitarise police functions.
- Replace Serbia's personal identification number **JMBG** with a new identifier, **LIB**, with free document replacement.
- Establish a permanent independent election commission, audit voter registers and publish precinct results.
- Introduce a **mixed electoral system** combining single-member districts and proportional party lists; permit electronic support signatures and restrict the use of state resources in campaigns.[^17]

### 16. Gender equality, parental leave and family policy

- Introduce transparent pay frameworks and stronger sanctions for discrimination.
- Allow a companion chosen by the mother to attend childbirth at no charge, guarantee return to the same job and change the calculation of maternity benefits.
- Extend care leave for the first and second child from **365 to 547 days, conditional on the father taking at least 182 days**; adapt the mechanism for some small-business owners and agricultural workers.
- Supply **free sanitary pads in public-institution toilets**; apply **10% VAT** to menstrual products and lower VAT on products for children under five.
- Introduce menstrual leave, with attention to severe symptoms and related disorders. This section **does not specify the number of days, pay rules or certification requirements**.
- Strengthen protection against coerced inheritance waivers, expand legal aid and publicly funded care services.
- Address obstetric violence, femicide, trafficking, forced marriage and conversion therapy.[^18]

### 17. Environmental protection

- Restore a dedicated environmental fund and independent impact assessment for major projects.
- Protect water resources, improve water networks and expand wastewater treatment.
- Publish pollution data, trigger inspection responses to excessive emissions and enforce industrial standards.
- Restrict hazardous waste, introduce packaging deposit systems and expand protected natural areas.
- Subsidise solar panels and building efficiency while gradually reducing coal-based emissions.[^19]

### 18. Sport

- Provide a sports voucher to every schoolchild, redeemable at accredited sports clubs.
- Restrict membership fees at subsidised clubs and prohibit charges for children transferring between clubs.
- Use specialist physical education teachers from the first grade.
- Establish an Olympic fund, licence coaches and provide independent protection against abuse in sport.[^20]

### 19. Tourism

- Publish registers of tourism subsidies and review investment outcomes.
- Prioritise infrastructure repairs over subsidising new hotels in overstretched destinations.
- Register and regulate short-term rentals; support health-resort and rural tourism.
- Distribute tourism vouchers according to social criteria and require paid student placements and properly registered work.[^21]

### 20. Students and youth

- Give grants and student loans fixed payment dates, harmonise travel discounts and make student canteen rules more flexible.
- Keep student housing available during summer.
- Recognise student plenums, permit independent candidacies for student parliaments and introduce confidential complaint systems.
- Require mentored, paid practical training and support youth councils in municipalities.[^22]

## Editorial assessment: unresolved implementation questions

The following are **editorial questions**, not assertions made by the Student List itself.

| Issue | What the programme sets out | Evidence needed to assess implementation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public finances | New spending targets, social benefits, state funds and tax reductions | A consolidated multi-year cost and revenue model, including downside scenarios |
| Employment | Additional employer obligations and stricter fixed-term contract rules | Effects on labour costs, hiring, small firms and employment |
| Public administration | More state services and central IT infrastructure, alongside promises to cut bureaucracy | Administrative costs, external oversight and centralisation risks |
| Parental leave | A 547-day extension depends on fathers taking 182 days | Household income effects, exemptions and interaction with employment insurance |
| Menstrual leave | The proposal is stated without detailed terms in the relevant section | Number of days, payment, medical certification and safeguards against hiring discrimination |
| Lustration | Individual proceedings may result in bans on holding office | Legal grounds, evidentiary standard, time limits, appeals and independent oversight |
| Higher education | The short plan speaks broadly of free education; the detailed section **considers** tuition abolition | Precise scope, deadlines and funding |
| Public housing | The short plan promises discounted **rental and purchase**; the detailed housing section says public flats **will not be sold** | Whether different housing stocks are intended, or the texts conflict |

Equal pay for equal work and protection against coercion in inheritance are matters already addressed in Serbian law. Assessing whether the proposals create *new rights* or strengthen enforcement requires comparison with the legislation in force; the programme alone does not establish this.

**Scope limitation:** no **single consolidated financial balance for the entire programme** was identified in the reviewed published sections. That does not demonstrate that none exists elsewhere, nor does it establish that individual measures cannot work.
